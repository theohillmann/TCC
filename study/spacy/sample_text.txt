Early on Monday, Dr. Rachel Thompson woke up feeling unusually dizzy. She reached for her glucose meter, worried her blood sugar might be low again. Rachel had been managing her type 2 diabetes for the last six years and relied on Metformin and occasionally Dapagliflozin to keep things under control. Her husband, Michael Thompson, reminded her to take her medications before breakfast, which she almost forgot in her anxious state.

Later that day, Rachel visited her friend Lucas Reed in Toronto, Canada. Lucas had recently been prescribed Victoza® after switching from Glimepiride due to persistent side effects. “It’s exhausting,” Lucas said. “Sometimes I just want to stop it all.” Rachel looked at him with concern. She had also struggled with burnout and even skipped medication during a trip to Brazil last year. “You remember what happened when I stopped taking it for three days? My blood sugar hit 280 mg/dL,” she said, her voice trembling.

As they talked, Rachel received a notification on her phone: https://mydiabetesportal.org/reminders — a new platform to help manage diabetes treatment routines. She sighed in relief. “This could really help me keep track,” she said, showing Lucas how it worked. The app even sent reminders via WhatsApp, including calls to action like "Drink water!" and "Time for Victoza!". She saved the number as +1 (416) 555-8972.

That evening, alone in her apartment, Rachel scrolled through her health log and felt a mix of pride and frustration. Managing diabetes was not just about medication, it was emotional — guilt, fear, and the loneliness of chronic illness all lingered. “We’re doing our best, Michael,” she whispered to herself, thinking of her husband again. She turned off the lights, hoping tomorrow would feel a little easier.
